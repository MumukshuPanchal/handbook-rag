# What is fixed before the last two measurements of the hardening

Written on 25 November 2026 at 23:18 India time, by the session that coordinates the work,
after a review of the pull request and before either measurement is run under version 2 of
the answering instructions. (The 40 limit questions have been asked under version 1, and 7
of those 39 answers judged; no figure of that run has been read beyond the counts of
replies and requests.)

## 1. The limit questions (`eval-limits`, both versions of the instructions)

The figure that counts is **answers with no statement faulted, out of the 40 questions**. A
question that got no answer counts against it, whatever the cause: a decline written by the
model, a reply read as the fixed form alone, a decline made by a check. The causes are
reported one by one. The share of judged answers that the judge faults is reported beside
it, and is not the headline: the version that declines more would look better by it.

The two versions are compared question by question, with an exact sign test on the
questions that differ. If it gives p of 0.05 or more, the documents say that no clear
difference was seen, whichever way the counts point.

## 2. The whole answer evaluation under version 2, against the baseline

165 questions: 132 the handbook answers, 33 it does not. The baseline answered 127 of the
132 and declined 31 of the 33.

**The hardening is not merged as it stands** if any of these holds; the result is reported
and its cause looked for:

a. Of the 132 questions the handbook answers, 121 or fewer get an answer (6 or more fewer
   than the baseline), counting a reply that a check declined or read as a decline as no
   answer.
b. "Right call" over the 165 is lower than the baseline's, and an exact sign test over the
   questions whose call changed gives p below 0.05.
c. The checks of the reply decline, or take words out of, 4 or more replies to questions
   the handbook answers whose own words, as the model wrote them, give the reference
   answer's fact. A reader decides that, from the words kept in the saved run.
d. "Supported by its sources" by version 1 of the judge, or "Correct by the reference", is
   lower than the baseline's, and an exact sign test over the answers whose verdict changed
   gives p below 0.05.
e. Of the 33 questions the handbook does not answer, 28 or fewer are declined (3 or more
   fewer than the baseline).

Otherwise it is merged, with every difference reported whichever way it points, and with
the time until the first text reaches the reader beside the baseline's: version 2 holds
text back until a citation follows it, and that wait is a cost of the hardening.

Why these numbers: the free model does not give the same reply twice, and at these sizes
two or three questions change between two runs of one assistant. The lines are drawn
beyond that. They are a judgement, made before the result is known.

## 3. If requests remain

A second reply for each of the 20 held-out cases under both versions (40 requests), to see
whether one reply a case explains the one case that got worse. It is reported as a second
round and never merged into the first.
