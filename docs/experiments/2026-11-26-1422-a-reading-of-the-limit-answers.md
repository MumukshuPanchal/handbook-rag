# A reading of the limit answers, decided on after the judge's figure was known

Written on 26 November 2026 at 14:22 India time, by the session that coordinates the work.

**Why.** By the rule fixed beforehand (the note of 23:18 on 25 November), the 40 limit
questions show a clear difference: 32 answers with no statement faulted under version 1 of
the instructions, 39 under version 2, 7 questions better and none worse, p = 0.016. The
session that built the hardening then read the eight faulted answers and the seven
questions that moved, and judged three of the judge's verdicts wrong by the judge's own
instructions; without those three the difference would be 4 against 0, p = 0.125. That
reading was made by a session that knew which version wrote which answer.

**What is done about it.** All 80 answers (40 questions, two versions) are read once more
by a model session that is given them mixed, under neutral names, and is told neither which
version wrote an answer nor what the judge said of it. For each answer it is given the
question, the passage with its title, the limit that the limit set's card names for that
passage and where it stands (in the title or in the text), and the reply as the person
sees it. It labels each:

- **kept**: the answer gives the limit, in any wording, in any of its statements; or the
  question itself asks about that very group, place, time, amount or case, so that the
  answer need not repeat it (the judge's own rule);
- **left out**: the answer gives the rule and not the limit, so that a reader would take the
  rule to hold more widely than the passage says;
- **no answer**: the reply declines or does not answer the question.

A weaker wording of the limit ("new team members" for "in their first year") is labelled
by whether a reader would take the rule to cover a case the passage excludes, and the
reader marks such a label as unsure.

**What is reported.** The judge's figure stays the one the note of 23:18 fixed, and it is
reported as fixed. Beside it: the reader's count of answers that keep the limit, of 40, for
each version; the questions that differ, with an exact two-sided sign test; how often the
reader and the judge agree; and each answer on which they differ. If the two figures point
different ways, the documents say so and claim no more than the weaker of the two.

This reading was decided on after the judge's figure was known, and after one reading of the
faulted answers by a session that was not blind. It cannot be called fixed beforehand, and
the documents must not call it so.
