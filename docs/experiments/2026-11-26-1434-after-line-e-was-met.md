## The cause that was found

Six replies to questions the handbook does not answer count as answers:

- **`u002` and `u024`** are one sentence each in the fixed form for a missing part ("The
  passages found do not say …"), with citation markers after it and nothing else. The code
  reads a reply made only of the fixed form as a decline when it cites nothing, and as an
  answer when it carries a citation. So the service called each of them an answer and
  listed sources under a reply that says the passages do not say.
- **`u010`, `u043` and `u047`** say in the fixed form that the passages do not say what was
  asked, and then give a related fact with a citation. Version 1 declined all three.
- **`u004`** is the same part of an answer that the baseline gave.

`u006`, which the baseline answered, is declined.

## What changes

One thing. A reply made only of pieces in the fixed form is not an answer, whether or not
it carries citation markers: it is counted as a decline, and no source is listed under it.
No instruction, no boundary and no other check changes.

This was decided by the session that had read the six replies. It knew that the change
moves `u002` and `u024`, and it had checked by code that no other saved reply of this pull
request is a cited reply made only of the fixed form. Read again with the change, the same
replies give 29 of 33 declined. **That figure is not a result under line e**: the change
was made knowing what it would give.

The three replies that say the passages do not say, and then add a related fact, stay
answers and so stay wrong calls. Nothing about how a call is scored changes.

## What this cannot show

33 questions, one reply each, on a model that does not write the same reply twice: two
runs can differ by two or three questions with no change at all. A confirmation that
passes shows that the first count was not far below the line once the two misread replies
are read as what they say. It does not show that version 2 declines as often as version 1:
on the first run it did not.
