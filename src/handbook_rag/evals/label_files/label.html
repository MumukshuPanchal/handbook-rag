The rubric in short

Part A: is the marked statement backed by the passages it cites?

1 Supported
Every fact it asserts is in the passages it cites, or follows directly from them. Rewording is fine, and so is leaving detail out.
2 Unsupported
At least one fact is not backed by the passages it cites: it is not in them, it differs from them, it is wider than them (a limit, a condition or an exception left out), they contradict it, or it asserts a fact and cites no passage.
3 No claim
It asserts nothing about what the handbook says: a heading, a lead-in such as "The steps are:", or a sentence saying that something is not covered.
4 Cannot tell
What is shown does not let you decide. Say why in the note.

Only the passages the statement cites count, titles included. Read the statement with the question and the statements before it. One unbacked fact is enough. "May" is not "must".

The rubric in full

Part B: does the answer tell the asker what the reference answer tells them?

1 Correct
It gives what the question asks for, as the reference answer gives it, in any wording, and nothing in it contradicts the reference answer.
2 Partial
What it says is right by the reference answer, but part of what the question asks for is missing.
3 Incorrect
Another fact, the opposite, other people or places, an answer to another question, no answer ("it depends", "ask your manager"), or the right fact beside one that contradicts the reference answer.
4 Cannot tell
What is shown does not let you decide. Say why in the note.

The reference answer is the only measure: do not use what you know yourself. The question decides what matters. More detail is fine unless it contradicts the reference answer.
