A search finds the passages, and a language model writes the answer from them, with the number of its passage after each statement. When the passages do not hold the answer, it says so.

What happens when you ask

  1. A follow-up is rewritten first. With earlier questions, one request to the model turns "and in Finland?" into a question that stands alone.
  2. A stored answer, when there is one for the question, is served in place of the rest, without a request, and names the question it was written for.
  3. The handbook is searched by meaning and by keyword, and the two lists are merged.
  4. A model writes the answer from the passages found, with a number after each statement. Code checks that every number names a passage that was sent.
  5. Or it declines when the passages do not hold the answer: with one fixed sentence, or in its own words that the passages do not say, with no sources.
